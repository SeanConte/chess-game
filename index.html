    Reference

    How Chess II Works

    Chess II is about controlling positions rather than attacking pieces. Each piece projects its normal chess attack pattern. A position is outnumbered when the opponent has more pieces covering it than you do. Players deploy pieces from a bank to build overlapping control, create threats, and reshape lines of support.

    Game modes

    • Sudden Death: if any of your pieces are outnumbered at the end of your turn, you lose.
    • Queens Gambit: outnumbered pieces are removed in cascading rounds. You lose when your last queen, on the board or in the bank, is gone.
    • Total War: outnumbered pieces are removed in cascading rounds. You lose when you have no pieces left on the board or in the bank.

    Movement

    • None: pieces stay where they are deployed. If both banks empty before another ending occurs, the player with more surviving pieces wins.
    • Line of sight: instead of deploying, move one board piece according to the squares it attacks under normal chess movement. The destination must be empty and not outnumbered.
    • Anywhere: instead of deploying, move one board piece to any empty square that is not outnumbered.

    No legal action: if you begin a turn with no legal placement or movement available, you lose.
